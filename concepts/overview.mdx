Kerne answers one question, asked on every request: **does this subject have the right to do that, right now, given what it pays?**

```
Subject wants an action  →  kerne.allows() / enforce() / check()
                         →  Kerne resolves: subscription → pack → entitlement → override
                         →  Your app reacts: proceed, or show an upgrade prompt
```

Everything else in the platform exists to make that one decision correct and fast.

## The two halves of the model

**Identity** - who is making the request. A user signs up, logs in, and holds a session. See [Identity & access](/concepts/identity).

**Monetization** - what that user is entitled to. A user subscribes to a `Plan`, which pins a versioned `Pack` (a snapshot of feature entitlements, frozen at subscription time) - see [Monetization](/concepts/monetization).

## Subject: the concept that decides fit

Your integration is one Kerne **tenant** - the boundary around your own account. You'll only ever have one; it's not something you configure or think about day to day.

What actually decides whether Kerne's model matches your product is **subject** - which of your own users or accounts a given entitlement belongs to. It's who `kerne.allows()`/`consume()`/`check()` are asked about. Today a subject is always exactly one `User` - in API terms, `subject_type` is always `"USER"`, and it's the SDK's `subject` parameter.

That fits cleanly if you bill and gate individual people: a support-ticketing SaaS charging per agent, a design tool where every seat logs in and pays on its own. It's not a native fit yet if what you bill is **a group as one unit** - "one company, twenty employees, one shared subscription, one pooled quota" - because there's no `subject_type: "ORGANIZATION"` today. The usual workaround is to designate one user (an account owner, a billing contact) as the subject the subscription attaches to, and build your own membership/seats logic on top in your own database - that works, but it's a workaround you build, not something Kerne represents natively yet.

If the thing your product bills is a company or a team rather than a person, know that before you build against Kerne - see [What's intentionally not built yet](#whats-intentionally-not-built-yet) below.

## What exists today

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Identity">
    Email/password and magic-link-style sessions, invitation-gated or open registration, a waitlist.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Catalog & entitlements">
    Products, plans versioned via packs - so upgrading a plan's entitlements never silently changes what existing subscribers get, see [grandfathering](/concepts/monetization/products-and-plans) - and three entitlement types: boolean flags, numeric quotas, and static config values.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Payments">
    Stripe is the payment rail to connect today (Polar also runs, for tenants connected before now). Kerne is always the source of truth for entitlements; the provider only handles money movement.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Admin">
    No per-user admin flag - admin operations on your tenant require your secret key, not a user session.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## What's intentionally not built yet

Being explicit about this matters more than it sounds - a doc that describes a half-built feature as if it were finished costs you more trust than one that says "not yet."

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Organizations / teams">
    A subject is always one user - see [Subject: the concept that decides fit](#subject-the-concept-that-decides-fit) above. Billing or gating a whole team or company as a single unit, with a pooled quota, isn't implemented.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Role-based access control">
    There's no per-user permission system at all - no custom roles, no per-resource permissions, no admin flag. Admin operations require your secret key.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="OAuth / social login">
    Only email+password and magic-link-style flows exist.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

If your product needs any of these today, it's worth knowing before you build against Kerne - not after.
