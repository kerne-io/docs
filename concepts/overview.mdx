Kerne sits between two things that change at different speeds: **what you sell**, which changes whenever the business decides it should, and **what your product does**, which you would rather not redeploy every time.

So Kerne holds the part in the middle — the rules that connect a customer's commercial situation to what they can actually do — and answers questions about them.

## What Kerne holds

Four kinds of rule, and they compose:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="What you sell" icon="tags">
    Your catalog: the plans, the prices, and what each plan includes. Configured once, versioned every
    time you change it.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What each customer bought" icon="receipt">
    Their subscription, the version of your offer it's attached to, and its current state — active,
    past due, canceling at the end of the period.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What they've used" icon="gauge">
    Counters for anything with an allowance: how much this period, against what limit, and what
    happens once it runs out.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What was agreed with them specifically" icon="user-pen">
    Overrides — the negotiated limit, the goodwill credit, the deal that exists for exactly one
    customer and isn't part of your catalog.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

None of that lives in your application. Your application asks.

## How a decision gets made

Your app names a **capability** — `export_pdf`, `api_calls`, `support_level` — and who it's asking about. Kerne resolves the rules that apply to that person, in this order:

```
Feature asked about
  → the subject's subscription (or your default plan, if they have none)
  → the version of the offer they bought
  → what that version grants for this feature
  → an override, if one exists for this subject
  → their usage counter, for anything with an allowance
  → the answer
```

Which comes back as one of three things, depending on what you asked for:

| | Hands back | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [`allows()`](/sdks/server/entitlements/allows-enforce-check) | `true` / `false` | The common case: gate an action |
| [`check()`](/sdks/server/entitlements/allows-enforce-check) | the whole picture — limit, used, remaining, why | Showing the customer where they stand |
| [`consume()`](/sdks/server/entitlements/consume) | the same, after counting the usage | When the action *is* the thing being metered |

The point isn't the shape of the call. It's that the answer changes when your pricing changes, when a customer upgrades, when a payment fails, or when you grant someone an exception — and none of those events touch your code.

## Who a decision is about

Every decision is about someone — the `subject` your SDK calls name. Today that's always one of your users, which is what makes Kerne a fit for products that gate individual people, and a partial fit for products that bill a company as one unit. [Is Kerne a fit?](/guides/is-kerne-a-fit) covers that in two minutes.
