---
title: 'Usage billing'
description: 'Charge for what people actually use, without ever charging more than you promised'
---

Most pricing is a flat monthly fee. Usage billing is the other half: your customer pays for what they consume, and you decide where that stops.

Kerne splits that into **three separate decisions**. They look similar and they are not, so it's worth being precise once:

<CardGroup cols={3}>
  <Card title="What's included" icon="gift">
    Free every period. `720` hours, `10,000` calls.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What it costs after" icon="receipt">
    A rate from your payment provider, and an optional **ceiling in money**.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Where it stops dead" icon="hand">
    A ceiling in **units**, which actually blocks. Optional, and rarely what you want.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The one that surprises people: **the money ceiling doesn't block anything.** Past it, your customer keeps working and you stop charging. That's deliberate — see [Why the cap doesn't block](#why-the-cap-doesnt-block).

## A real example

A company rents out macOS machines. Their offer: **0.22 € per hour of machine time, never more than 150 € a month per customer.**

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a metered price at Stripe">
    A **meter** that counts events, and a **recurring price** attached to it. Kerne reads the rate from that price — you never type an amount into Kerne.

    Set the price to `0.22 €` per **60 units**, and meter in minutes. Not `0.0037 € per minute`: 0.22 divided by 60 doesn't have an exact decimal, and rounding it would drift away from what Stripe actually invoices.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the feature">
    Type **Quota**, unit `minute`, reset **each period**.

    A billed quota must reset. If it rolled over, last month's consumption would count as billable again this month — Kerne refuses the setup rather than let that happen.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set the entitlement on the plan">
    Included: `0`. At the limit: **Bill**. Pick the metered price. Billing cap: `150`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Report usage from your backend">
    ```typescript
    await kerne.entitlements.consume('machine_minutes', {
      scope: customerId,
      delta: 60, // one hour
    });
    ```
  </Step>
</Steps>

That's it. Kerne counts, and a background worker hands the billable minutes to Stripe. Your customer's invoice grows to 150 € and stops.

## Per machine, per project, per anything

The example above pools every machine into one counter. Often that's exactly right — the customer is billed for total consumption, and which machine produced it is their business.

Sometimes it isn't. **Dimensions** let one quota split into several counters under the same customer:

```typescript
await kerne.entitlements.consume('machine_minutes', {
  scope: customerId,
  dimension: 'machine_42', // ← this
  delta: 60,
});
```

Declare what a feature is counted by (`machine`) on the **feature**, then each plan chooses what that means:

| Allowance | What your customer gets |
| --- | --- |
| **Shared across every machine** | One pool. 200 hours total, whoever burns them. |
| **Each machine gets its own** | 200 hours *per machine*, counted separately. Machine A running out doesn't touch machine B. |

And the cap has its own, independent choice — because these two promise opposite things:

| Cap | What you're promising |
| --- | --- |
| **Total for the customer** | "Your bill never exceeds 150 €." Reassuring for them, and it caps what you earn however much they grow. |
| **Per machine** | "Each machine costs you at most 150 €." Ten machines can bill ten times it — and the cost of adding one more is known in advance, on both sides. |

<Note>
  Allowance and cap bind independently: "shared pool, one cap" and "own allowance per machine, own cap per machine" are both valid, and so are the two mixes.
</Note>

Attribution works either way: even on a shared pool, every usage event records which machine it came from, so "which one cost me the most" stays answerable.

## Why the cap doesn't block

This is the part worth understanding, because it's the opposite of what most systems do.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Money and access are separate">
    Hitting a spending cap means *you* stop charging. Your customer notices nothing. Their machines keep running, their API keeps answering.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Blocking is its own setting">
    If you genuinely want consumption to stop, that's the **hard stop** — a ceiling in units, and it's off by default.
  </Card>
</Columns>

The reason is simple: a spending cap is a promise you made about *your invoice*. Turning it into an outage would punish the customer for a promise you made to reassure them.

## What you see

The **Usage billing** screen shows every subscriber on a billed entitlement: consumed, billable, already charged, and how much room is left under the cap.

It also shows two things nothing else would tell you:

- **Capped** — still consuming, no longer charged. Working as designed, but you want to know.
- **Not billing** — the provider refused this entitlement's usage (its price was archived, its meter was turned off). Access still works, revenue is silently zero. This is the failure this screen exists for.

<Note>
  The numbers on that screen come from the same function the billing worker uses. What you read is what gets invoiced, not a parallel estimate that can drift.
</Note>

## Things that will bite you

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Meter in the smallest unit, always">
    Minutes, not hours. Bytes, not gigabytes. Kerne counts in whole numbers — that's what makes concurrent consumption safe — so a fractional hour has nowhere to go. Same discipline as storing money in cents.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The rate is a pair, not a number">
    "0.22 € per 60 minutes" is not "0.0037 € per minute". Stripe divides, rounds the block count, *then* multiplies — computing it the other way disagrees by up to a full block, always in the direction that exceeds your cap.

    You never enter a rate in Kerne, precisely so this can't go wrong.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="A cap is a block count, not a division">
    A 150 € cap at 0.22 € per hour doesn't buy 681.8 hours. It buys **681 whole hours**, because the 682nd would round up and invoice 150.04 €. Kerne computes it the provider's way.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Tiered prices work, but can't be capped">
    Per-unit or tiered — Kerne pushes units and Stripe prices them. But a spending cap is inverted into a unit ceiling ("how many hours fit under 150 €?"), and a tier schedule has no single rate to invert. So: tiered without a cap, or per-unit with one.

    On a tiered price the dashboard shows "tiered pricing, set at Stripe" rather than inventing a rate, and the billed amount reads from your Stripe invoice.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="If you archive the metered price at Stripe">
    Existing subscribers keep billing on it, but nobody new can subscribe to that plan. Kerne flags the entitlement as **Not billing** so you find out from your dashboard rather than from a support ticket.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
