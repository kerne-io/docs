You sell one or more plans. Each costs something, and each includes something. You build that in the dashboard, under **Catalog**, and it looks like what it is.

What the dashboard can't decide for you is what happens on the day you change it.

## Changing your offer without changing your customers

Say you raise the Pro plan's limit from 200 to 500, and add a feature to it. Two things could happen:

- everyone already on Pro gets the new terms, or
- they keep what they signed up for, and only new customers see the change.

Both are legitimate. A phone carrier usually leaves existing customers on their old plan when the lineup changes; a car insurer reprices everyone at renewal. What's not legitimate is the third option: _finding out which one happened after it happened_.

So Kerne asks you, every time you save a change to a plan:

- **Update this offer**: everyone on it gets the change, today's subscribers included.
- **Start selling to new subscribers**: the change becomes a new version of the offer, sold to new buyers only. Everyone already subscribed keeps the version they bought, renewal included.

```
Version 1 (on sale until v2)   → Customer A stays on v1, even after v2 goes on sale
Version 2 (on sale after that) → Customer B, who subscribes later, gets v2
```

Your code never mentions a version. `allows()`, `check()` and `entitlements()` resolve through whichever one the customer is on. **Offers**, next to the product's name in the editor, lists every version and how many customers are on each.

Prices don't ask: a new price applies to new checkouts, and Stripe keeps charging existing subscribers what they signed up for.

## Moving someone to a newer version

Two things do it, both deliberate:

- **They change plan.** They get the new plan's current version and price.
- **You move them.** In **Offers**, under **Move customers**, from one version of a plan to another. It changes what they get, not what they pay.

Nothing else moves a customer between versions. To change what every version includes at once, including the ones customers kept, use **Update rights on every offer**: see [Change your pricing](/guides/billing/change-your-pricing#offers).

## Cancellations and failed payments

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="They cancel">
    **End of period** by default: access continues through what's already paid for, and stops when
    that period ends. Passing `immediately` to `cancel()` cuts access right away instead. That's a
    choice per call, not a stored setting.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Their payment fails">
    By default, Kerne doesn't cut access over a failed payment. The subscription is `PAST_DUE` while
    Stripe retries. When the retries run out, your Stripe settings decide what happens: if Stripe
    cancels the subscription or marks it unpaid, the customer falls back to your **Default** plan.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

`PAST_DUE` and `UNPAID` are subscription statuses: see the [subscription shape](/sdks/server/billing#read-subscriptions) for the full list.

A plan change applies immediately, upgrade or downgrade. There's no end-of-period option yet.
