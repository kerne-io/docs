---
title: 'Is Kerne right for me?'
description: 'Where the model fits, and the boundaries worth knowing first'
---

Kerne fits one shape well: one payer, one account, one set of rules about what they can do. It stops fitting the moment several people need different access inside that same account — that part isn't built yet.

## It fits when

- Someone signs up and pays for their own access — a person, or a company where one account covers everyone who works there
- Your plan checks are scattered across the codebase, and you'd rather ask one question than go hunt them all down every time pricing changes
- Your pricing is going to change, and existing customers shouldn't silently lose what they already paid for
- You're on Stripe and want it to move the money while something else decides access
- You charge for usage and don't want to build the metering yourself

That covers most per-seat SaaS, usage-based APIs and AI products, good-better-best tiers, and B2B sold as one account per company.

## Current boundaries

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Several people sharing one account">
    A company can be a customer on its own — no user required. What's missing is **membership**:
    several of your users acting on that same company's account from a login session. Today,
    access is one-to-one between a session and the customer it names; only your own backend,
    calling with your secret key, can act on a customer on someone else's behalf.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Roles and resource-level permissions">
    No per-user roles, no "this teammate can bill but not delete." Administering your tenant
    requires your secret key, not a logged-in user's session — see [Admin access](/concepts/identity/admin-access).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Social login">
    Email + password and magic link only. If social login is required at launch, keep identity in
    your own stack and use Kerne for access instead.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Catalog from code">
    Products, plans and prices are dashboard-only. There's no SDK call to create them.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## The simple test

If the question is *"Can this customer do X right now?"* — Kerne is the right layer.

If the question is *"What can these specific people, with these specific roles, do inside this shared account?"* — that part isn't built yet.
