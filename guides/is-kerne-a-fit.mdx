---
title: 'Is Kerne right for me?'
description: 'When the model matches your product'
---

Kerne fits when **the thing you sell to and the thing you gate are the same individual person**. Almost everything else is negotiable.

## Where it fits well

- You sell to individual users — or you're comfortable making one user the billing contact for an account.
- You'd rather write `allows('export_pdf')` than `if (user.plan === 'pro')`, and have pricing decisions stop living in your codebase.
- Your pricing **will change**, and existing customers should keep what they bought unless you say otherwise.
- You bill through Stripe, and you want Kerne to own access while the provider owns the money.
- You want metered pricing — real rates, tiers, spending caps, optional per-project or per-seat counters — without building the metering yourself.
- Email + password and magic link are enough authentication for now, or you already own identity elsewhere.

Typical strong fits: per-seat SaaS, usage-based API or AI products, good-better-best tiers with on/off and allowance features.

## Current boundaries

These are deliberate, and they're the things worth knowing before you design around the model:

| Boundary | What it means for you |
| --- | --- |
| **A subject is one user** | Billing a company as one unit, with a pooled allowance, isn't modelled. Make one user the billing contact and keep membership in your own database. |
| **No organizations or teams** | Same root cause as above. |
| **No roles or per-resource permissions** | "This teammate can manage billing but not delete projects" stays in your application. Administering your tenant uses your secret key — see [Admin access](/concepts/identity/admin-access). |
| **Email + password and magic link only** | No social or OAuth sign-in. If that's a launch requirement, keep identity in your own stack and use Kerne for access. |
| **Subjects are Kerne user ids** | You can't attach a subscription to an arbitrary external id; mirror your users into Kerne instead. |
| **Catalog lives in the dashboard** | Products, plans and prices are configured there, not created from the server SDK. |

## The practical test

If your core loop is *"this person, under this commercial situation, may do X right now"*, Kerne is the right layer.

If it's *"this company, with these roles, under this shared pool"*, you'll be fighting the model.
