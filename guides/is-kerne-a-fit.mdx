---
title: 'Is Kerne a fit?'
description: "Where the model matches your product — and where it doesn't yet"
---

Kerne makes a few choices deliberately, and two or three of them decide whether it suits your product at all. Better to find out here than three weeks into an integration.

The short version: **Kerne fits when the thing you sell to, and the thing you gate, are the same individual person.** Almost everything else is negotiable.

## Where it fits well

Most of these will be true:

- You sell **to individual users** — or you're comfortable designating one user as the billing subject for an account.
- You'd rather write `allows('export_pdf')` than `if (user.plan === 'pro')`, and have pricing decisions stop living in your codebase.
- Your pricing **will change**, and you want existing customers to keep what they bought unless you say otherwise.
- You bill through **Stripe** (or already use Polar), and you want Kerne to own access while the provider owns the money.
- You want **usage-based pricing** — real rates, tiers, spending caps, optional per-project or per-seat counters — without building the metering yourself.
- **Email + password and magic link** are enough authentication for now, or you already own identity elsewhere.

In that shape the model stays small: one subject, one subscription, a versioned set of entitlements, and one decision per gated action.

## Where it isn't a native fit yet

These are boundaries, not a to-do list. They matter more than any feature comparison.

### Organizations and teams as the billing unit

A **subject is always one user**. There's no `subject_type: "ORGANIZATION"`, and no pooled allowance owned by a company as a first-class object.

If what you sell is "one company, twenty seats, one subscription, one shared allowance", you'll either designate one user (owner, billing contact) as the subject and keep membership in your own database, or wait for native organization subjects. The first is a workaround you own; the second doesn't exist today.

### Social and OAuth login

**Email + password** and **magic link** only. No Google, no GitHub, no OAuth provider integration — the `oauth_providers` field on auth config exists but is always empty.

If social login is a launch requirement, keep identity in your own stack and use Kerne for access instead.

### Fine-grained roles and permissions

There's no per-user RBAC, no custom roles, no admin flag on a session. Administering your tenant requires your **secret key**, not a logged-in user's JWT — see [Admin access](/concepts/identity/admin-access).

"This teammate can manage billing but not delete the project" lives in your application for now.

### External subjects without a Kerne user

You can't attach a subscription or a counter to an arbitrary external id (Clerk, Auth0, Firebase). The subject in `allows()` / `consume()` is always a Kerne user id.

The workaround is to create a Kerne user mirroring your external identity and use that id as the subject. It works; it isn't a pure external-subject model.

## Common product shapes

| What you sell | Fit today | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Per-seat SaaS where each person logs in and pays (or is invited under one payer you model yourself) | Strong | Subject = user is natural |
| Usage-based API, AI, or infrastructure (tokens, minutes, messages) | Strong | Meters, rates, caps and dimensions are first-class |
| Good-better-best plans with on/off and allowance features | Strong | Versioned packs are built for exactly this |
| B2B with one company subscription and pooled allowances | Partial | Needs a designated billing user and your own membership model |
| A product that already uses Clerk or Auth0 and only wants entitlements | Partial | Possible via mirrored users; not a pure external subject |
| A consumer app that needs "Sign in with Google" on day one | Weak until OAuth ships | Identity is the blocker, not monetization |

## The practical test

If your core loop is **"this person, under this commercial situation, may do X right now"**, Kerne is the right layer.

If it's **"this company, with these roles, under this shared pool, signed in with Google"**, you'll be fighting the model or waiting on the roadmap.

The rest of the docs assume the first shape, and call out the second wherever it would otherwise surprise you.
