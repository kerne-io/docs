---
title: 'Handle customer exceptions'
description: 'Different limits for one customer, without touching your catalog'
---

A customer negotiates a limit nobody else gets. Another needs a temporary bump while you fix something. A third deserves a credit after an outage.

None of these should become a plan. Give that customer an exception, and your existing checks answer correctly for them. The API calls it an [override](/concepts/monetization/overrides).

## From the dashboard

Open **Customers**, pick the customer, then the **Exceptions** tab, and click **New exception**. An exception replaces what their plan gives them on one feature. It doesn't add to it.

## A negotiated limit

From your backend, for an annual contract with terms your public pricing doesn't offer:

```typescript
await kerne.admin.grant('api_calls', {
  customer: userId,
  value: '50000',
  reason: 'Annual contract',
  expiresAt: '2027-01-01T00:00:00.000Z', // omit for no end date
});
```

This replaces their limit, and renews every period like the plan's own would. Set `expiresAt` to the contract's end date, so the deal doesn't quietly outlive it.

## A bump on one counter

On a feature counted per machine, per project or per seat, you can change one counter and leave the rest on the plan's limit:

```typescript
await kerne.admin.grant('machine_minutes', {
  customer: userId,
  dimension: 'machine_42',
  value: '180',
  reason: 'Loaner machine while theirs is repaired',
});
```

The most specific exception wins, so "every machine at 3 hours, this one at 5" is two grants, not one per machine.

## A one-time credit

This is the one people get wrong. A refund is **not** a limit change: raising their limit would give them the same gift every period.

```typescript
await kerne.admin.adjustUsage('api_calls', { customer: userId, delta: -5000 });
```

That moves their counter once. Pass an `idempotencyKey` if the call might be retried.

## Cleaning up

```typescript
const grants = await kerne.admin.listGrants(userId);
await kerne.admin.revokeGrant(grants[0].id);
```

Expired exceptions stay in the list, so you keep a record of what was promised to whom.

<Warning>
  `kerne.admin` needs your secret key. Call it from your back office, never from a request an end
  user controls. Exceptions also need to be on your Kerne plan, or `grant()` fails with `K5002`.
</Warning>

## What an exception won't do

It changes the limit, not what happens past it. Whether going over stops, stays free or gets billed is always read from the plan. A different behavior for one customer means a different plan.
